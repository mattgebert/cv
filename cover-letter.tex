    \pind My current role is a postdoctoral research fellow in the Materials Science Engineering department at Monash University, under the supervision of Prof. Christopher McNeill, who is an expert in using synchrotron radiation to study photovoltaic materials. Through this role I have conducted over 15 beamtimes across facilities such as the Australian Synchrotron, the National Synchrotron Light Source II (NSLS-II) in the US, and the Swiss Light Source (SLS) in Switzerland. I have developed a deep interest in the control of beamline acquisition and investigation of materials, especially under in-situ conditions. For example, the Australian Synchrotron and NSLS-II utilize the \cvreference{Bluesky}{https://github.com/bluesky/bluesky}control system, a modern open source framework for repeatable experimentation. Such dynamic experiments include blade coating to measure drying phenomena around the blade edge, and in-situ annealing experiments. Thermal expansion, multi-kinetic motion and time sensitivity all contribute to control challenges in such experiments. These experiments are conducted with teams including doctoral students, where I take a leadership role in training and guiding students. Additionally, I have also been developing modern software open-source python toolkits to enable easy interaction and analysis of beamline experiments (see  repositories of\cvreference{XraySoftMat}{https://github.com/xraysoftmat/}github). While these tools are not directly related to control and automation, they demonstrate my calibre ability in software development, to meet modern software-engineering standards (i.e. unit-testing, semantic versioning etc.). I believe these skills are essential for modern control and automation research.
    
    \pind Complementing my postdoctoral experience, I have a PhD in condensed matter physics from Monash University under the supervision of Prof. Michael Fuhrer, researching surface science of 2D and topological materials and their influence to material proximity. I developed expertise in growth and transfer methods for 2D crystals, another area where there is urgent need for improvements in automation and control to produce high quality heterostructures. My doctoral experience as part of FLEET has also allowed me to grow a diverse research network in novel materials and systems across Australia. %Dirac surface materials such as graphene.

The emerging field of liquid-metals offer many new experimental possibilities (ex-situ and in-situ) in surface science and spectroscopy, again offering opportunities for control and automation innovation. In my postdoctoral role I am working in new collaborations across multiple existing projects with colleagues at European Synchrotrons (such as the ESRF and SLS) with recently travelled in Oct. 2025.
