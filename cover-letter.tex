As a postdoctoral researcher at Monash university with extensive experience in synchrotron-based X-ray spectroscopy and scattering, scientific computing and software development, I am very excited by the opportunity to support and enable the scientific computing mission of the Australian Synchrotron in multi-disciplinary context.

    My education is quite diverse, and serves well to relate and support the variety of stakeholders that are involved in the work at the Synchrotron. 
    My current role is a postdoctoral research fellow under Prof. Chris McNeill in MSE at Monash University, with extensive experience in spectroscopy and diffraction beamline science. A primary part of this work has been building and maintaining scientific software packages to model and process large experimental datasets. 
    Complementing my postdoc, I have a PhD in condensed matter physics under Prof. Michael Fuhrer, where I utilised a wide range of technologies including electronic acquisition and UHV/cryogenic control systems to probe the electronic properties of 2D materials, including neutron reflectometry at the Lucas Heights ACNS upon which  Andrew Nelson's RefNX software package exposed me to the importance of robust and maintainable software in scientific research.
    My honours degree in electrical engineering  further cements my technical background with a strong understanding of electronics, instrumentation, and control systems.
    The breadth of my technical background demonstrates my ability to comprehensively understand new technology, which would make me a valuable contributor to the development of new scientific computing tools and workflows at the Australian Synchrotron.

    My work has led to a strong interest in scientific software particularly oriented to reproducible user workflows. I have been developing open-source Python toolkits for experimental analysis of beamline experiments (see\cvreference{XraySoftMat}{https://github.com/xraysoftmat/}repositories). For example,\cvreference{`XEFI'}{https://github.com/xraysoftmat/XEFI}simulates the x-ray electric field intensity waveguided in thin films, implementing complex electromagnetic field calculations that matches the physics in research papers.
    I have also developed\cvreference{pyNexafs}{https://github.com/xraysoftmat/pyNexafs}, which implements a full tested workflow for reducing and analysing NEXAFS Spectroscopy datasets, particularly being mindful of incompatibility between file formats as equipment is upgraded and replaced.
    Through these projects I have developed a strong experience in software engineering, including continuous integration and testing, semantic versioning, and documentation. They also demonstrate my ability to identify projects that are useful to the scientific community.
    I believe these skills are essential for modern research in user-focused facilities such as the Australian Synchrotron, and particularly useful in the context of high-turnover x-ray tools, where repetitive dataset processing is required.
