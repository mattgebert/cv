    \pind I am currently a postdoctoral research fellow in the Department of Materials Science Engineering under the supervision of Prof. Christopher McNeill. My research focuses on synchrotron-based characterisation of photovoltaic materials, in which I have conducted numerous beamtimes across facilities such as the National Synchrotron Light Source II, USA. My work has led to a deep interest in beamline control and in-situ materials investigation, especially for in-situ experiments, where processes such as blade coating and thermal annealing must be monitored and adapted in real time. These experiments require tailored control and automation solutions, which I implement during facility time for our teams.
    I have gained familiarity with control systems such as\cvreference{Bluesky}{https://github.com/bluesky/bluesky}(a modern open source framework), which are utilised for repeatable experimentation.
    
    \pind In parallel, I have been developing open-source Python toolkits for interaction and analysis of beamline experiments (see\cvreference{XraySoftMat}{https://github.com/xraysoftmat/}repositories). While not control systems themselves, these tools contribute to reproducible, extensible and integrated experimental workflows. They also demonstrate strong software development capability, to meet modern software-engineering standards (i.e. unit-testing, semantic versioning etc.). I believe these skills are essential for modern control and automation research.
    
    \pind Complementarily, my condensed matter physics doctorate (supervised by Prof. Michael Fuhrer) investigated surface and interface phenomena in 2D and topological materials, work which required precise control over crystal growth and transfer processes in cryogenic and vacuum-based systems. These systems also have clear opportunities for automation in achieving reproducible, high-quality heterostructures.  %Dirac surface materials such as graphene.

The emerging field of liquid metals offer many new experimental possibilities, again generating strong opportunities for control and automation innovation. I have developed national networks (such as FLEET) and am internationally engaged in collaborations with colleagues at European institutes, including recent travel in Oct. 2025 supported by an ECR Grant.
